1-Mark Questions (Objective & Factual Recall)
What is the primary function of the bibliographic header in a book review? Answer: To record critical publication metadata (title, author, publisher, publication year, total pages, price, and ISBN) so prospective readers can identify and procure the text.
What does the term "spoiler" mean in book review etiquette? Answer: The premature disclosure of major plot twists, climaxes, or narrative endings that deprives prospective readers of the reading experience.
A book review is strictly an objective summary with no personal opinions. (True or False) Answer: False (A review requires critical evaluation and subjective analytical judgment alongside summary).
Name the standard concluding component of an academic book review. Answer: Target audience recommendation and final rating.
2-Mark Questions (Short Concepts & Distinctions)
Distinguish between a book summary and a book review. Answer:
Book Summary: An objective, descriptive recapitulation of plot or arguments without personal judgment.
Book Review: A critical appraisal that analyzes literary craft, evaluates strengths and limitations, and assesses whether the author fulfilled their intended purpose.
Mention two criteria used to evaluate non-fiction prose in a book review. Answer: (i) The logical validity, factual accuracy, and credibility of the author’s thesis, and (ii) The clarity, structural organization, and readability of the prose style.
What is the function of the "Hook" in a book review's opening paragraph? Answer: To engage reader attention using an intriguing question, notable quotation, or striking statement while introducing the book's core premise and authorial context.

1-Mark Questions (Objective & AI Literacy)
What does the term "AI Hallucination" mean in generative language models? Answer: A phenomenon where an AI model generates factually incorrect statements, fake citations, or non-existent quotes while presenting them with plausible authority.
Submitting an AI-generated essay as your own original work is considered a form of academic plagiarism. (True or False) Answer: True.
What does the acronym 'HITL' stand for in ethical AI-assisted communication? Answer: Human-in-the-Loop.
Inputting confidential corporate records or private personal data into commercial AI chatbots can cause data privacy breaches. (True or False) Answer: True.
2-Mark Questions (Short Concepts & Ethical Dilemmas)
What is 'Algorithmic Bias' in Generative AI text production? Answer: Systematic prejudices or cultural stereotypes present in an AI tool's training data that cause the model to generate biased or discriminatory outputs.
State two ethical boundaries students must respect when using AI for academic assignments. Answer: (i) Use AI tools for preliminary brainstorming, outlining, and structural refinement rather than generating entire essays, and (ii) Fact-check every citation, statistic, and quote against primary scholarly literature.
Why is human editing necessary when using AI translation or writing tools? Answer: To correct factual errors (hallucinations), adjust unnatural phrasing, ensure appropriate cultural context, and preserve an authentic human voice.
5-Mark Analytical Note: Ethical Guidelines for AI Use
Write a short note on the ethical guidelines undergraduate students should follow when using Artificial Intelligence tools for writing assignments.

Artificial Intelligence writing assistants can support learning when used responsibly, but students must follow clear ethical boundaries:

Brainstorming over Drafting: AI tools should be used to outline topics, summarize background readings, or refine grammatical structures, rather than generating entire essays.
Mandatory Fact-Checking: Generative AI tools often hallucinate historical quotes, legal precedents, and scholarly sources. Students must verify every claim against peer-reviewed books and journals.
Transparent Disclosure: When university guidelines permit AI use, students should acknowledge the tool and prompts used in their methodology or reference notes.
Preserving Original Voice: Higher education builds critical thinking. Relying entirely on automated text prevents students from developing their own analytical voice. Human writers must remain responsible for the final ideas, interpretations, and integrity of their work.

Unit 1: Developing Professional Writing Skills
Chapter 1: Book Review Writing
1. Definition, Purpose & Critical Function
Definition: A book review is a critical evaluation of a written work that combines an analytical summary of its core content with an objective assessment of its strengths, limitations, and overall contribution to its field or genre.
Primary Objective: To guide prospective readers on whether the work warrants their time and financial investment, while evaluating how successfully the author fulfilled their stated thesis.
Book Summary vs. Book Review:
Summary: A descriptive, objective condensation of plot, arguments, and content with zero subjective judgment or critical evaluation.
Review: An evaluative appraisal that analyzes literary craft, assesses factual validity, interrogates methodology, and offers a personal verdict.
2. Standard Five-Part Architecture of a Book Review
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│             BOOK REVIEW STRUCTURAL ANATOMY             │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Bibliographic Header                                │
│    • Title, Author, Publisher, Year, Pages, Price, ISBN│
│ 2. Introduction (The Hook & Context)                   │
│    • Opening statement, author profile, thesis         │
3. Thematic / Narrative Summary                          │
│    • Core premise, structure (No Spoilers)             │
│ 4. Critical Assessment & Literary Appraisal            │
│    • Prose style, pacing, factual evidence, flaws      │
│ 5. Conclusion, Audience Recommendation & Rating        │
│    • Target readership, final value verdict, rating    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
Bibliographic Header: Records essential publication metadata at the top: Full Title, Author, Publisher, Year of Publication, Edition, Total Page Count, Retail Price, and ISBN.
Introduction (The Hook & Thesis): Engages the reader using an intriguing quote, striking observation, or thematic question. Introduces the author’s background, historical context, and the reviewer’s thesis statement regarding the work's overall success.
Thematic Summary (Spoiler-Free Overview): Condenses the central narrative arc (for fiction/autobiography) or core theoretical arguments (for non-fiction). The Golden Rule: No Spoilers. Avoid revealing major plot twists, turning points, or endings.
Critical Assessment & Stylistic Evaluation: The analytical core of the review, evaluating:
Thematic Depth & Originality: Does the author present fresh perspectives or repeat clichés?
Prose Craft & Pacing: Is the language accessible, lyrical, dense, or jargon-laden? Does the pacing drag?
Evidence & Research: For non-fiction, are claims backed by verified documentation, primary archives, or data?
Defects & Limitations: Identify weak character development, unexamined biases, or gaps in argumentation.
Conclusion, Recommendation & Rating: Summarizes the final impression. Explicitly identifies the target readership (e.g., general readers, undergraduates, academic specialists) and assigns a clear numerical or qualitative rating (e.g., 4.5/5 stars).
3. Evaluation Criteria for Fiction vs. Non-Fiction
Dimension	Fiction Review Metrics	Non-Fiction Review Metrics
Primary Focus	Character development, plot coherence, emotional resonance, narrative voice.	Thesis clarity, empirical evidence, logical consistency, research rigor.
Pacing & Style	Descriptive imagery, dialogue authenticity, suspense, structural balance.	Readability, structural organization, density of specialized jargon.
Contribution	Aesthetic originality, subversion of genre tropes, emotional depth.	Contribution to existing scholarship, public discourse, practical applicability.
Chapter 2: Right to Information (RTI) Applications
1. Legal Background & Core Statutory Provisions
The Right to Information Act, 2005: Enacted by the Parliament of India to promote transparency, accountability, and containment of corruption in public authorities. It grants Indian citizens the legal power to inspect public works, access certified documents, and obtain official records.
Public Authority (Section 2(h)): Any authority, body, or institution established:
By or under the Constitution of India;
By any Central or State law;
By government notification, including non-governmental organizations substantially financed by government funds.
Public Information Officer (PIO / CPIO): The designated administrative officer in every public authority responsible for receiving applications, procuring information from relevant wings, and delivering it to the applicant.
Statutory Timelines (Section 7(1)):
Standard Queries: Information must be provided within 30 days of application receipt.
Involvement of Assistant PIO (APIO): Timeline extends to 35 days.
Life or Liberty of a Person: Information must be provided within 48 hours.
Fee Structure:
Standard application fee: ₹10 (paid via Indian Postal Order [IPO], Demand Draft, or court fee stamp).
BPL Exemption: Citizens below the poverty line are exempt from all application and photocopying fees (must enclose a certified copy of a valid BPL card).
Appellate Mechanisms:
First Appeal (Section 19(1)): Filed within 30 days of deadline expiry or receipt of an adverse order, addressed to the First Appellate Authority (FAA) (an officer senior in rank to the PIO).
Second Appeal (Section 19(3)): Filed within 90 days of the FAA's decision before the State Information Commission (SIC) or Central Information Commission (CIC).

                                     │

                                     │

                                     │
                     [If dissatisfied with FAA order]

2. Drafting Strategy & Rules
Target Specific Records: Request physical, recorded documentation (e.g., “certified copies of work orders, measurement books, tender notices”).
Avoid Interrogative "Why" Questions: The RTI Act allows access to recorded data, not administrative justifications.
Incorrect: “Why did the engineer not fix the road?”
Correct: “Certified copy of the file notes and inspection reports regarding the repair of Road X.”
Keep Questions Numbered and Precise: Structure requests into distinct points rather than long paragraphs.

1. Document Typology: Resume vs. CV vs. Bio-Data
Evaluation Metric	Resume	Curriculum Vitae (CV)	Bio-Data
Etymology	French: Résumé (“summary”).	Latin: Course of Life.	English: Biographical Data.
Primary Scope	Private sector, corporate roles, technical jobs.	Academia, scientific research, fellowships, medical roles.	Government clerkships, military defense, matrimonial profiles.
Standard Length	1 to 2 pages maximum.	Unlimited (3 to 10+ pages).	1 to 2 pages.
Core Emphasis	Specific job skills, achievements, measurable outcomes.	Complete academic record, publications, conferences, grants.	Personal details (gender, religion, date of birth, marital status).
Customization	Tailored to each specific job description.	Comprehensive record; updated as accomplishments occur.	Static, standardized biographical form.
2. Resume Structural Formats
                           RESUME FORMAT CLASSIFICATION
                                        │
     ┌──────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                                  ▼                                  ▼
CHRONOLOGICAL FORMAT             FUNCTIONAL FORMAT                  COMBINATION / HYBRID
• Reverse chronological timeline • Skills-based clusters            • Combines skills summary
• Best for stable career growth  • Hides employment gaps / pivots    with chronological history
Reverse-Chronological Format:
Lists education and employment history from most recent to earliest.
Best suited for: Candidates with a steady, linear career progression in the same field.
Preferred by automated Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) and corporate recruiters.
Functional (Skills-Based) Format:
Groups qualifications under functional skill categories (e.g., "Editorial Management," "Digital Media Strategy") rather than chronological dates.
Best suited for: Professionals with employment gaps or candidates changing industries.
Combination (Hybrid) Format:
Features a prominent skills summary at the top, followed by a reverse-chronological work history.
Best suited for: Experienced professionals seeking to highlight specialized competencies while maintaining a verifiable employment record.
3. Writing Effective Achievement Bullet Points
The Action-Verb Formula: Start each bullet point with a strong past-tense action verb (e.g., Formulated, Coordinated, Spearheaded, Systematized).
The Google X-Y-Z Metric: “Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z].”
Weak: “Helped with editing student articles.”
Strong (X-Y-Z): “Edited 45 academic manuscripts [X], reducing typographical errors by 30% [Y], by implementing a standardized three-pass proofreading checklist [Z].”
4. Cover Letter: The Three-Tier Architecture
A formal cover letter must accompany a resume, acting as a tailored pitch that links the applicant's credentials directly to the employer's needs.

Salutation: Address the hiring manager by name (“Dear Dr. Baruah,” “Dear Ms. Hazarika”). Use “Dear Hiring Manager” only if the name is unavailable. Avoid “To Whom It May Concern”.
Paragraph 1 (The Hook & Intent): State the exact position applied for, the job reference code, and a brief statement of qualification.
Paragraph 2 (The Match & Evidence): Connect your specific background to the role’s requirements. Highlight 2–3 specific accomplishments using evidence and numbers.
Paragraph 3 (The Actionable Close): Reaffirm enthusiasm for the organization, request an interview, and reference the enclosed resume.
Chapter 4: Professional & Technical Formats
1. Formal Business Letters
Format Structure:
Sender's Letterhead / Address: Top left or centered.
Dateline: Fully written out (e.g., 21 September 2026, never 21/9/26).
Reference Number: Tracks correspondence in corporate filing systems.
Inside Address: Name, designation, and company address of the recipient.
Subject Line: Concise, bolded statement of purpose (under 10 words).
Salutation: Formal greeting (“Dear Sir/Madam,”).
Body: Three-paragraph structure (Opening statement, core details/evidence, expected remedy/action).
Complimentary Close: “Yours faithfully” (if recipient is unnamed) or “Yours sincerely” (if named).
Signature Block: Handwritten signature, typed full name, and designation.
Types of Formal Business Letters:
Letter of Inquiry: Requests detailed information, catalogs, price quotations, or service terms.
Letter of Complaint: Details a contractual breach, defective delivery, or service shortfall, requesting replacement or a refund.
Placing an Order: Lists items, specifications, unit prices, shipping instructions, and agreed payment terms.
2. Official Memoranda (Memos)
Purpose: Internal communication within the same company or department to announce policy updates, assign duties, or document operational directives.
Key Features: Direct, concise, and operational. Memos omit formal inside addresses and complimentary closes.

3. Public Notices
Purpose: Public announcements designed to share important information with a broad audience within an institution.
Key Format Rules:
Must be enclosed within a boxed frame.
The word NOTICE must appear centered in bold capital letters below the institutional name.
Must include the issue date, an informative title, clear body text covering What, When, Where, Who, and Why, and the issuing authority's signature.
4. Technical Incident & Event Reports
Purpose: An objective, factual account of an event, project milestone, or workplace accident.
Core Style Guidelines:
Objective Third-Person Voice: Use passive constructions (“The fire was detected,” “The session was inaugurated”); avoid personal pronouns (I, we).
Chronological Structure: Document events sequentially from initiation to conclusion.
Factual Precision: Use verified data, specific times, names, and measurable costs.
Actionable Recommendations: Conclude incident reports with specific preventive measures.

1. Architectural Anatomy of a Workplace Email

The Subject Line: The primary filter determining whether an email is opened. It must be specific, searchable, and professional.
Poor: “Quick question,” “Hey,” “Urgent”
Professional: “Request for Leave: Aniket Sharma (Roll 45),” “Minutes: Curriculum Committee Meeting (Sept 21)”
The Recipient Fields:
To: The individuals from whom direct action or a response is required.
Cc (Carbon Copy): Individuals who need to stay informed, but are not required to take direct action.
Bcc (Blind Carbon Copy): Conceals recipient email addresses from everyone else. Used to protect privacy when sending messages to large groups, or for discreet record-keeping.
Salutations & Sign-Offs:
Formal Salutations: “Dear Professor Baruah,” “Dear Mr. Goswami,” “Dear Selection Committee,”
Casual Salutations (Avoid in Business): “Hey folks,” “Hi there,” “Yo”
Professional Sign-offs: “Sincerely,” “Respectfully,” “Best regards,” “Warm regards,”
Inappropriate Sign-offs: “Cheers,” “Thanks a bunch,” “Yours lovingly”
2. Netiquette: The Rules of Digital Email Etiquette
Avoid ALL CAPS: Typing in capital letters is read as digital screaming.
The "Reply All" Rule: Use Reply All only if your response is essential to every recipient on the thread. For personal acknowledgments (“Thank you, got it”), reply only to the sender.
Double-Check Attachments: Always confirm that files are attached and correctly named before hitting send.
Maintain Professional Spacing: Use single spacing within paragraphs and a double space between paragraphs. Avoid informal slang, acronyms (BTW, ASAP), and casual emojis.
Chapter 6: Microblogging & Social Media Communication
1. Platform Dynamics & Writing Constraints
Definition: Microblogging involves publishing short, direct messages on platforms like X (formerly Twitter) and Threads to inform, engage, and drive traffic to external resources.
Character Economy: Standard posts on X are limited to 280 characters. Writers must make every word count by using active verbs, eliminating filler adjectives, and using concise phrasing.
The Inverted Pyramid Model: Place the most critical information (who, what, when, where) in the opening sentence, followed by supporting details, links, and hashtags.
                  THE INVERTED PYRAMID IN MICROBLOGGING
                  ┌───────────────────────────────────┐
                  │ 1. THE LEAD (Core Fact / Event)   │
                  ├───────────────────────────────────┤
                  │ 2. Context & Actionable Links     │
                  ├───────────────────────────────────┤
                  │ 3. Category Hashtags & Handles    │
                  └───────────────────────────────────┘
2. Social Media Mechanics & Strategy
Hashtag (#) Usage:
Functions as a search index to group related conversations across the platform.
Best Practice: Use 1 to 3 targeted hashtags per post. Cluttering posts with excessive hashtags reduces engagement and readability.
Account Tagging (@): Mentions other accounts to direct messages, credit sources, or alert institutional partners.
Thread Architecture: A linked series of posts used to unpack complex stories or multi-step announcements that exceed single-post character limits.
Brand Voice: The consistent personality, tone, vocabulary, and values an organization maintains across its public channels (e.g., academic, authoritative, accessible, or empathetic).
3. Crisis Communication on Social Media
When an institutional mistake, service disruption, or controversy occurs:

Acknowledge Quickly with Verified Facts: Silence allows rumors to spread. Post verified information promptly.
Never Delete Critical Comments Arbitrarily: Deleting public criticism looks defensive and damages institutional credibility.
Keep Tone Empathetic and Professional: Avoid arguing with users. Direct complex disputes to private channels (“Please DM our team with your application ID so we can resolve this directly”).

Chapter 8: Ethical Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Communication
1. Generative AI Writing Tools & Mechanics
Generative AI (Large Language Models / LLMs): Machine-learning systems (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) trained on large text datasets. They predict and generate natural, human-like text based on user prompts.
Common Use Cases: Brainstorming topics, generating structural outlines, summarizing long background readings, and checking grammar.
2. The Four Major Ethical Traps
                           THE FOUR ETHICAL TRAPS OF AI
                                        │
     ┌──────────────────┬───────────────┴───────────────┬──────────────────┐
     ▼                  ▼                               ▼                  ▼
AI HALLUCINATIONS      AUTOMATED PLAGIARISM            ALGORITHMIC BIAS    DATA PRIVACY LEAKS
Invented citations,    Passing off AI-generated        Repeating societal  Feeding private records
fake quotes & facts    text as original writing        stereotypes         into commercial models
AI Hallucinations:
A phenomenon where generative language models produce completely fabricated facts, non-existent book titles, or fake citations while presenting them with plausible authority.
Ethical Imperative: Writers must manually verify every quote, date, and source against primary scholarly literature.
Automated Plagiarism & Academic Misconduct:
Copy-pasting AI-generated text into assignments, reports, or articles without attribution violates copyright norms and institutional academic integrity policies.
Algorithmic Bias:
AI systems can reflect the cultural, racial, and gender biases present in their training data. Uncritical use can unintentionally reinforce historical stereotypes.
Data Privacy & Confidentiality Breaches:
Feeding sensitive corporate reports, patient records, or unreleased academic research into public commercial AI chatbots can compromise private data, as commercial tools often use prompt history for further model training.
3. The Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Framework
The ethical standard for professional and academic writing requires maintaining Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) oversight:

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│             THE HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP WORKFLOW             │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Conception & Ideation : Human identifies goals.     │
│ 2. AI Brainstorming      : Generates rough outlines.   │
│ 3. Human Drafting        : Writes original content.    │
│ 4. AI Polish (Grammar)   : Suggests style edits.       │
│ 5. Human Verification    : Fact-checks all claims.     │
│ 6. Full Accountability   : Human signs the final work. │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
Clear Ethical Boundaries for Students & Professionals:
Permitted: Using AI to generate structural outlines, brainstorm ideas, rephrase clumsy sentences, and check basic grammar.
Prohibited: Using AI to draft complete essays, generate literary analysis, or create fake academic citations.
Full Attribution: Acknowledge AI tool use transparently in project methodology notes or reference pages whenever used.
Human Accountability: The human author remains fully responsible for the factual accuracy, logical consistency, and ethical integrity of the final submitted text.
