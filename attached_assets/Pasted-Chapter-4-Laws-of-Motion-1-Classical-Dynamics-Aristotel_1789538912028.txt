The Aristotelian Fallacy vs. Galilean Inertia
 * Aristotle's View: Stated that an external force is required to keep a body in uniform motion (e.g., an arrow stays in flight because air rushes behind it to push it forward).
 * The Galilean Resolution: Moving bodies on Earth naturally come to rest because of opposing resistive forces: dry contact friction, viscous drag, and air resistance. On Earth, an applied force is required solely to counteract these resistive forces so that the net external force sums to zero:
   
   
   In an ideal world free of all friction, a body initiated into horizontal motion continues to travel at constant velocity indefinitely without any propulsion.

 * Galileo's Double-Incline Thought Experiment:
   * A sphere rolling down an inclined plane accelerates, gaining speed.
   * Rolling up an opposing incline retards the sphere until it reaches its original release height h.
   * Lowering the slope of the upward incline forces the ball to travel a longer distance along the plane to reach height h.
   * In the limit where the opposing slope is zero (a horizontal plane), the ball must travel an infinite distance to recover its initial height—its motion never ceases.
 * Definition of Inertia: The inherent resistance of any material body to change its state of rest or uniform rectilinear motion. Inertial mass (m) is the quantitative scalar measure of inertia.

 * Physical Meaning of Equilibrium:
   * Zero acceleration does not imply the absence of forces; it means all external forces balance out.
   * Internal forces between constituent particles of a body always cancel in pairs and cannot accelerate the body's center of mass.
Manifestations of Inertia & Everyday Mechanics
 * Inertia of Rest: When a stationary vehicle accelerates forward abruptly, the feet of standing passengers accelerate forward through floor friction, while the upper torso remains behind due to inertia, causing passengers to jerk backward relative to the vehicle.
 * Inertia of Motion: When a speeding vehicle brakes, the lower limbs decelerate with the chassis, while the upper torso continues moving forward at the previous speed due to inertia, pitching passengers forward.
 * Inertia of Direction: Mud particles thrown from a spinning vehicle tire travel tangentially to the tire perimeter because cohesive forces cannot supply the inward centripetal acceleration needed to keep them on the curve.
 * Locomotion in Empty Space: A horse cannot pull a cart in a vacuum. Forward motion requires the horse to exert an inclined backward-downward contact force against the ground, which generates an equal and opposite reaction force whose horizontal component provides forward thrust. In empty space, there is no external surface to supply this reaction force.

Conceptual Diagnostic: Mechanical Causes of Terrestrial Locomotion and Forward Inertia [Textbook Exercise 4.23 (a, b)]
 * (a) Why a horse cannot pull a cart in empty space:
   To accelerate forward, a horse pushes backward and downward against the ground. By Newton's third law, the ground pushes forward and upward on the horse. The forward horizontal component of this reaction force propels the horse and cart. In empty space, there is no surface to push against, so no external reaction force can be generated, making locomotion impossible.
 * (b) Why passengers are pitched forward when a bus stops suddenly:
   Before braking, the passenger's entire body moves forward at the bus's cruising speed. When brakes are applied, friction between the floor and the passenger's feet brings their lower body to rest along with the chassis. Because the body is flexible, the upper torso continues moving forward at the previous velocity due to inertia of motion, pitching the passenger forward.

Clarifying Misconceptions About Action and Reaction
> Newton's Third Law: To every action, there is always an equal and opposite reaction.
> 
 * "Action" and "Reaction" are Forces: These terms refer strictly to simultaneous mechanical forces.
 * Simultaneity: Action and reaction occur at the same instant. There is no cause-and-effect time delay; either force can be called the action, and the other the reaction.
 * Forces Act on Different Bodies: If body A exerts a force \vec{F}_{BA} on body B, then body B simultaneously exerts a force \vec{F}_{AB} = -\vec{F}_{BA} on body A:
   
   
   Because action and reaction act on separate bodies, they never balance or cancel each other to produce equilibrium on a single body. They cancel only when both bodies are grouped together into a single combined system.

 * Similarly, the force \vec{F}_{BA} exerted on B by A produces a change in momentum in B:
