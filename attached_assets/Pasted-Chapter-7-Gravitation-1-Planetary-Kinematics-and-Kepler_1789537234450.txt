1. Planetary Kinematics and Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion

Newton later showed that Kepler's laws are a direct mathematical consequence of the inverse-square law of universal gravitation and the conservation of angular momentum.

 * First Law (Law of Orbits): Every planet revolves around the Sun in an elliptical orbit, with the Sun situated at one of the two foci of the ellipse.

Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation states that every mass particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them:


where G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N m}^2\text{kg}^{-2} is the universal gravitational constant, determined experimentally by Henry Cavendish using a torsion balance.
Newton's Shell Theorems
 * Shell Theorem I (Point Mass Outside a Uniform Spherical Shell): The gravitational attraction exerted by a thin, uniform spherical shell on an external point mass is identical to the force that would arise if the entire mass of the shell were concentrated at its geometric center.

 * Shell Theorem II (Point Mass Inside a Uniform Spherical Shell): The gravitational force exerted by a uniform spherical shell on a point mass placed anywhere inside it is identically zero.
   * Mechanism: At any interior point P, decomposing the shell into opposing double cones reveals that the surface area (and thus mass) of a patch scales with r^2, while the force per unit mass scales with \frac{1}{r^2}. The geometric factors cancel, leaving the net force from opposing patches balanced in every direction.

Conceptual Diagnostic: Gravitational Shielding, Extended Frames, and Tidal Forces [Textbook Exercise 7.1]
 * (a) Gravitational Shielding:
   Unlike electrostatic forces—where mobile charges in a conductor redistribute to produce an interior field \vec{E} = \vec{0}—gravitational mass is strictly positive, and there are no negative gravitational charges. Gravitational attraction depends only on the intervening masses and cannot be shielded by enclosing a body within a hollow shell or other barrier.
 * (b) Gravity Detection in Large Space Stations:
   In a small spacecraft, all parts fall with essentially identical gravitational acceleration, rendering the local frame weightless. However, in a very large space station, the gradient of gravity (\frac{\partial g}{\partial r} \propto \frac{1}{r^3}) produces measurable differences in acceleration between the near and far ends of the structure, generating observable tidal stresses.
 * (c) Tidal Force Disparity (Moon vs. Sun):
   The absolute gravitational pull of the Sun on Earth is roughly 180 times stronger than that of the Moon (F \propto \frac{M}{r^2}). However, tidal effects depend on the differential gradient of gravitational force across Earth's diameter (2R_E):
   
   
   Because the Moon is much closer to Earth than the Sun, the factor \left(\frac{r_S}{r_M}\right)^3 \approx (389)^3 \approx 5.8 \times 10^7 outweighs the Sun's larger mass ratio (\frac{M_\odot}{M_M} \approx 2.7 \times 10^7). Consequently, the Moon's tidal force is approximately twice as strong as that of the Sun.

Conceptual Diagnostic: Satellite Energetics and Atmospheric Drag Paradox [Textbook Exercises 7.6 & 5.5(c)]
 * Total Energy vs. Energies: With potential energy defined as zero at infinity, the total mechanical energy of an orbiting satellite is the negative of its kinetic energy (E = -K) and equals half its potential energy (E = \frac{U}{2}).
 * Escape Work for Orbiting vs. Stationary Body:
   * For an orbiting satellite: E_{\text{orbit}} = -\frac{G M_E m}{2r}, so energy required to escape is \Delta E_{\text{escape}} = +\frac{G M_E m}{2r}.
   * For a stationary object at the same altitude: E_{\text{stat}} = -\frac{G M_E m}{r}, so energy required to escape is \Delta E = +\frac{G M_E m}{r}.
   * Therefore, launching an orbiting satellite to infinity requires less energy than projecting a stationary object from the same height, because the satellite already possesses orbital kinetic energy.
 * The Atmospheric Drag Paradox: As a satellite in low Earth orbit experiences frictional drag from the thin upper atmosphere, its total mechanical energy gradually dissipates (E becomes more negative). Because E = -\frac{G M_E m}{2r}, a decrease in E forces the orbital radius r to shrink. Since orbital speed is v_o = \sqrt{\frac{G M_E}{r}}, a smaller radius leads to an increased orbital speed. The loss of potential energy exceeds the energy dissipated by friction, with the remainder converting into kinetic energy.

The Physical Nature of Weightlessness
Weight is defined as the contact reaction force exerted by a supporting floor or balance on a body (W_{\text{apparent}} = N).
In an orbiting spacecraft, both the astronaut and the vessel fall freely with the identical gravitational acceleration (a = g(r)):


The sensation of weightlessness does not arise from the absence of gravity; at orbital altitudes of 400\text{ km}, Earth's gravitational field remains roughly 89\% of its surface strength. Weightlessness is entirely a consequence of continuous unconstrained free fall.
Analytical Case Studies & Dynamic Evaluations
Conceptual Diagnostic: Physics and Physiological Symptoms of Weightlessness [PYQ 2023 Final & Textbook Exercise 7.9]
 * Step 1: System Isolation: Analyze an astronaut orbiting Earth in a microgravity environment.
 * Step 2: Symptom Diagnostic Breakdown:
   * (a) Swollen feet: Unlikely. On Earth, gravity causes blood and fluids to pool in the lower extremities. In microgravity, fluids redistribute upward toward the thoracic and cranial cavities, reducing foot swelling while causing facial puffiness.
   * (b) Swollen face: Likely. Fluid shifts toward the head increase facial volume and nasal congestion.
   * (c) Headache: Likely. Fluid shifts increase intracranial pressure, commonly causing headaches during adaptation.
   * (d) Orientational problems: Likely. The vestibular system in the inner ear relies on gravity to signal "up" and "down". Without this stimulus, spatial disorientation (space motion sickness) is common.
 * Step 3: Explicit Final Verification: An astronaut in orbit is most likely to experience swollen face, headache, and orientational problems; swollen feet do not occur.
