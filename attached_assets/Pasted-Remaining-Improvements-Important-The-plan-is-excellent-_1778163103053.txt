Remaining Improvements (Important)

The plan is excellent,
but there are still a few important refinements.

⚠️ 1. Incident Timeline Auto-Narrative Needs Rules

Right now:

incident timeline (auto-narrative)

could become noisy.

Recommendation:

Define strict narrative rules.

Example:
Only include:

state transitions,
alerts,
fallback spikes,
latency spikes > threshold,
canary failures.

Otherwise timelines become unreadable at scale.

⚠️ 2. Circuit State Derivation Needs Hysteresis

Without hysteresis:
providers may flap between:

closed,
half_open,
open.
Recommendation:

Explicit thresholds:

Transition	Rule
closed → half_open	3 failed canaries
half_open → open	failure persists 5m
open → closed	10 consecutive healthy checks

This prevents alert storms.

Very important.

⚠️ 3. Synthetic Journeys Should NOT Hit Real Billing Paths

Important operational detail.

For:

Razorpay,
SES,
ElevenLabs,
Deepgram,

ensure:

sandbox/test keys,
billing-safe routes,
or synthetic bypasses.

Otherwise canaries become expensive/noisy.

⚠️ 4. Emergency Operator Console Needs “Reason Required”

VERY important.

Every toggle should require:

reason,
incident ID,
operator identity.

Example:

Disable embeddings
Reason: Pinecone outage INC-204
TTL: 1h

This matters later for:

audits,
debugging,
postmortems.
⚠️ 5. Blast Radius Estimation Should Be Explicitly Approximate

This line:

~N paid users affected next hour

is good,
BUT:
operators may overtrust it.

Recommendation:

Label clearly as:

“estimated operational impact”

not authoritative forecasting.

⚠️ 6. Provider Health UI Needs “Last Updated”

Simple but important.

At incident time,
operators need confidence:

telemetry itself isn’t stale.

Add:

last event processed: X sec ago
⚠️ 7. Mongo TTL Collections Need Capacity Planning

You’re storing:

canaries,
spans,
incidents,
timelines.

At 100K DAU:
this grows FAST.

Recommendation:

Document retention policy NOW.

Example:

Data	Retention
Raw spans	7d
Aggregates	90d
Incidents	1y
SLA ledgers	permanent

Otherwise observability becomes your largest database unexpectedly.
