Classification of Work by Sign
                       SIGNS OF MECHANICAL WORK
                                   |
         +-------------------------+-------------------------+
         |                         |                         |
   POSITIVE WORK              ZERO WORK                 NEGATIVE WORK
   0° <= θ < 90°              θ = 90° or d = 0          90° < θ <= 180°
   cos θ > 0                  cos θ = 0                 cos θ < 0
   Energy supplied to body[span_24](start_span)[span_24](end_span)   No mechanical transfer[span_25](start_span)[span_25](end_span)   Energy removed from body[span_26](start_span)[span_26](end_span)

 * Positive Work (W > 0): Occurs when the component of force aligns with the direction of displacement (0 \le \theta < \frac{\pi}{2}). Examples include lifting a load via an upward tension force or a vehicle accelerating under engine thrust.
 * Zero Work (W = 0): Occurs when:
   * Net displacement vanishes (\vec{d} = \vec{0}), even if the applied force is large (e.g., pushing firmly against an immovable concrete wall).
   * The applied force is zero (\vec{F} = \vec{0}).
   * The force is strictly perpendicular to the displacement (\theta = \frac{\pi}{2} \implies \cos 90^\circ = 0). Examples include gravitational pull on a planet in a circular orbit, normal reaction on a horizontal sliding block, or a porter carrying a load on a level floor.
 * Negative Work (W < 0): Occurs when the force opposes the direction of displacement (\frac{\pi}{2} < \theta \le \pi). Examples include kinetic friction opposing sliding motion (\theta = 180^\circ \implies W = -f_k d) or gravity acting on a body ascending vertically.

Conceptual Diagnostic: Classification of Potential Energy Profiles [Textbook Exercise 5.3]
 * General Diagnostic Principle:
   A particle with total energy E cannot exist in regions where V(x) > E, because that would require negative kinetic energy (K < 0). The minimum required total energy to exist anywhere in the potential is E_{\text{min}} = V_{\text{min}}.
 * Profile-by-Profile Analysis (Fig. 5.11):
   * (a) Square Well Potential (V = 0 for 0 \le x \le a, and V = V_0 > E elsewhere):
     The particle cannot enter regions x < 0 and x > a. Minimum total energy: \mathbf{E_{\text{min}} = 0}. Physical context: A bead sliding along a wire between rigid stops.
   * (b) Potential Step/Well with Varying Shelves (V_0 > E across parts of x):
     The particle cannot exist where V(x) > E. Minimum total energy: \mathbf{E_{\text{min}} = V_1} (the lowest potential shelf).
   * (c) Symmetric Harmonic Well (V(x) \propto x^2):
     Turning points occur at x = \pm x_0 where V(x_0) = E. The particle is forbidden from |x| > x_0. Minimum total energy: \mathbf{E_{\text{min}} = 0} at x = 0. Physical context: Mass-spring oscillator.
   * (d) Periodic Barrier Potential:
     Motion is confined to valleys where V(x) \le E; barrier peaks where V > E are forbidden. Minimum total energy: \mathbf{E_{\text{min}} = -V_1}. Physical context: Electron moving in a periodic crystal lattice.

 * (c) Satellite Orbital Decay Speed Paradox:
   As atmospheric drag slowly siphons mechanical energy from an orbiting satellite, its total energy decreases (E = -\frac{G M_E m}{2r} becomes more negative). This forces the orbital radius r to shrink. Because orbital velocity is v_o = \sqrt{\frac{G M_E}{r}}, a smaller radius causes the satellite's speed to progressively increase. The loss in potential energy is twice the gain in kinetic energy, with the difference dissipated as frictional heat.
 * (d) Direct Theory Alternatives [Textbook Exercise 5.6]:
   * When a conservative force does positive work, potential energy decreases (\Delta V = -W).
   * Work done by a body against friction results in a loss of its kinetic energy.
   * The rate of change of total momentum is proportional to the external force.
   * In an inelastic collision, total linear momentum and total energy remain unchanged, but kinetic energy changes.

Conceptual Diagnostic: Conservation Properties Across Collision Regimes [Textbook Exercises 5.7, 5.8, 5.14]
 * Exercise 5.7 Diagnostics:
   * (a) Momentum and energy of each body are conserved in an elastic collision: False. Total momentum and total energy of the combined system are conserved, but the colliding bodies exchange momentum and energy with each other.
   * (b) Total energy of a system is always conserved regardless of internal/external forces: False. Total energy is conserved only for an isolated system (\vec{F}_{\text{ext}} = \vec{0}). External forces can do work on the system, altering its total energy.
   * (c) Closed-loop work is zero for every force in nature: False. This holds only for conservative forces; non-conservative forces like friction and viscosity dissipate energy over closed paths (\oint \vec{f}_k \cdot d\vec{r} \ne 0).
   * (d) In an inelastic collision, final kinetic energy is always less than initial kinetic energy: True. Part of the initial kinetic energy is converted into non-mechanical forms (heat, sound, and permanent deformation).
 * Exercise 5.8 Diagnostics:
   * (a) Total kinetic energy during the collision contact interval: Not conserved. During contact, colliding bodies deform like compressed springs, temporarily storing part of their kinetic energy as elastic potential energy. The initial kinetic energy is recovered only after separation.
   * (b) Total linear momentum during contact: Strictly conserved. At every instant during impact, internal interaction forces balance in pairs (\vec{F}_{12} = -\vec{F}_{21}), maintaining total momentum conservation throughout.
   * (c) Inelastic collisions: Linear momentum remains conserved at every instant, but kinetic energy is not conserved during or after impact.
   * (d) Potential energy depending only on center-to-center separation distance: The collision is elastic. If the interaction potential depends solely on separation distance (V = V(r)), the interaction force is conservative, which ensures total kinetic energy is conserved after separation.
 * Gas Molecule Rebound [Exercise 5.14]:
   A molecule strikes a wall at 200\text{ m s}^{-1} and rebounds at the same speed.
